My first post is not about programming. It is about the habit that made the rest of my career possible: being willing to take an opportunity before I could know exactly where it would lead.

## My first job

I was eight years old when I started delivering newspapers in upstate New York.

The paper route was originally my older brother's idea. His route was enormous, so I took half of it. This was not the movie version where two brothers ride together and throw papers from their bicycles. He took one side of the street, I took the other, and eight-year-old me worked alone.

I woke up with the sun and delivered in rain, shine, and snow. There was a lot of snow. I was too small to balance a bicycle while carrying two full newspaper sacks, so I walked.

I did not go looking for that job, but I accepted the opportunity in front of me. Since then, there have not been many periods in my life when I did not work. I have had good jobs and bad ones. Some of the bad ones took years to recognize for what they were.

That history taught me something about opportunity: comfort can make a decision feel safer than it actually is.

## When good enough becomes a ceiling

You might not hate your job. You might love the people around you. It may be a position that many people would be grateful to have.

It still may not be where you are meant to stay.

A job can provide ninety percent of what you want while keeping the final ten percent permanently out of reach. The missing part might be growth, meaningful work, autonomy, location, compensation, or simply the feeling that you are becoming the person you intended to become.

There is nothing wrong with choosing stability. Stability can be a responsibility, especially when other people depend on you. The danger is allowing fear to make that choice on your behalf and then calling it contentment.

## A leap is not a guarantee

The next opportunity may be worse.

A role can sound perfect during an interview and reveal itself differently once you arrive. A manager can change. The work can become something other than what was promised. Taking a risk does not make the outcome fair.

But a bad choice is not always a permanent destination. It can teach you:

- Which warning signs you ignored.
- Which questions you should ask next time.
- Which parts of a role matter more than you expected.
- What you are no longer willing to trade away.
- Whether you can recover when a plan does not work.

Those lessons become part of your judgment. A setback can narrow the distance between the opportunity you imagine and the one that is actually right for you.

## Make the risk survivable

Faith does not require carelessness.

Before taking a leap, understand what the landing could cost. Save what you can. Talk honestly with the people affected by the decision. Research the opportunity. Ask uncomfortable questions. Know which consequences are temporary and which ones you cannot accept.

The goal is not to remove uncertainty. If certainty were available, it would not feel like a leap. The goal is to keep a disappointing outcome from becoming an unrecoverable one.

Calculated risk leaves room for courage without pretending that responsibilities do not exist.

## Do not reject yourself first

People often close the door before anyone else has the chance.

They do not apply because they lack one qualification. They do not ask for the role because someone else appears more ready. They do not leave because the current situation is familiar. They imagine the worst possible outcome and treat it as though it has already happened.

An opportunity can still say no. Let it.

Do not perform the rejection on its behalf.

## Take the step you can defend

I do not believe every dissatisfaction requires an immediate exit, or that every risk is noble. I believe there are moments when staying still carries its own cost, even if that cost is easier to ignore.

When an opportunity moves you toward the life you want, when you understand the risk, and when fear is the strongest reason to remain where you are, take the step.

You may land exactly where you hoped. You may learn that you need another leap. Either way, you will have chosen deliberately instead of allowing comfort to choose for you.
